What we treat

Everyday illness, long-term conditions, and the check-ups in between

If something needs a specialist, Dr. Mukherjee will say so and write a referral. He will not keep you waiting on a problem he cannot fix here.

  • Fever, cold and infections

    Viral fever, dengue and malaria season, throat and chest infections, stomach upsets, typhoid follow-up.

  • Blood pressure and heart

    Pressure checks, medicine adjustment, cholesterol reports, ECG in the chamber when needed.

  • Diabetes care

    Sugar testing, HbA1c review, diet advice in plain words, insulin guidance for those who need it.

  • Children and teenagers

    Fever, worms, poor appetite, growth worries, school health certificates. Vaccination advice.

  • Elderly care

    Joint pain, weakness, memory worries, multiple medicines checked together so nothing clashes.

  • Reports and check-ups

    Blood sugar and pressure done in the chamber. Full blood tests collected here by a tie-up lab, reports in two days.

Before you come

What to bring with you

Five minutes of preparation at home saves a second visit. Bring what you have; if you have none of these, come anyway.

  • Old prescriptions from any doctor, even if the problem seems unrelated.
  • Recent test reports, blood tests, X-rays, USG, in the original or on your phone.
  • The medicines you take now, or a photo of the strips. Names matter more than you think.
  • A note of your symptoms: when it started, what makes it worse, any fever readings.
  • For sugar tests, come fasting in the morning shift. Water is fine.
  • For children, the vaccination card and school health form if there is one.

How a visit goes

No appointment. Four simple steps.

  1. Take a token

    Come in during chamber hours. Tell the desk your name and the problem in one line. You get a number.

  2. Pressure and weight

    The assistant notes your blood pressure, weight and temperature while you wait. This is included in the fee.

  3. See the doctor

    Usually 10 to 15 minutes. He examines, reads your reports, and explains what he thinks is going on.

  4. Prescription and next step

    A written prescription with generic names where possible, and a clear note on when to come back, if at all.

Common questions

Do I need an appointment?
No. Walk in during chamber hours and take a token at the desk. If you are coming from far, send a WhatsApp message first and the desk will tell you how long the queue is.
Does the doctor do home visits?
Only for bed-ridden patients already under his care, within Kalighat, Bhowanipore and Hazra, and only after the evening chamber. The fee is ₹1,200. Call the desk to arrange it.
Can I show reports on WhatsApp?
You can send them so they are ready when you come, but the doctor will not advise on WhatsApp alone. A short visit is needed to examine you.
Is there a lift or ramp?
The chamber is on the ground floor with one small step at the entrance. A folding ramp is kept at the desk for wheelchairs.
Which languages does the doctor speak?
Bengali, Hindi and English. Prescriptions are written in English with the dose explained in your language.
Is a pharmacy nearby?
There are three medicine shops within 200 metres on Rashbehari Avenue. The chamber does not sell medicines and has no tie-up with any shop.
