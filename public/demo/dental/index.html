Treatments

What we do here, in plain words.

Before any work starts you will know what it is, how many sittings it takes and what it costs. If a case needs a specialist we do not have, we say so and refer you.

01

Scaling, polishing and fillings

Scaling removes the hard tartar a brush cannot. It takes about 30 minutes and does not hurt, though gums can feel tender for a day. We suggest it once a year, twice if you chew paan or smoke.

Fillings are tooth-coloured. A small cavity is done in one sitting. You can eat after an hour.

  • 1 sitting
  • 30 to 45 min
  • From ₹800
02

Root canal treatment (RCT) and crowns

When decay reaches the nerve, the tooth aches at night and with hot or cold. RCT cleans the inside of the tooth and seals it, so you keep the tooth instead of pulling it. It is done under local anaesthesia and is not the painful thing people remember from twenty years ago.

Most front teeth take two sittings, back teeth two or three. A crown (cap) goes on top to protect the tooth. Metal-ceramic and full ceramic options are on the fee list.

  • 2 to 3 sittings
  • Local anaesthesia
  • RCT from ₹3,500
03

Braces for children and adults

Crooked or crowded teeth, gaps, or an upper jaw that sticks out. Braces move teeth slowly over 12 to 24 months. Metal braces cost the least; ceramic braces are less visible. We also arrange clear aligners for suitable cases.

The best age to check a child is around 7 to 8, even if nothing is done until later. The first visit is a check and a plan, with a written cost before you decide.

  • 12 to 24 months
  • Monthly visits
  • Metal from ₹28,000
04

Extraction and implants

A tooth that cannot be saved is removed under local anaesthesia. Wisdom teeth that are stuck take a little longer and we tell you in advance. You go home the same day with written instructions.

An implant replaces a missing tooth with a titanium root and a crown. It needs healthy bone and three to four months of healing between the two stages. Bridges and dentures are the lower-cost options and we explain both.

  • Same-day extraction
  • Implant in 2 stages
  • Implant from ₹25,000
05

Children's dentistry

The first visit for a child is just a look and a ride in the chair. No drill. We want the second visit to be easy. Milk teeth do get filled when needed because they hold the space for the adult teeth.

Fluoride application and pit-and-fissure sealants cut down cavities in school-going children. Both are quick and painless. Dr. Anindita Sen sees most of the children.

  • From age 3
  • Evening slots for school children
  • Check-up ₹300

Your first visit

What happens, step by step.

Set aside about 40 minutes. Nothing is drilled on the first visit unless you are in pain and ask for it.

  1. 0 to 5 min

    Your name and the problem

    Tell the desk what brought you. If you have old X-rays or a prescription, show them. Give the name of any medicine you take, especially for sugar, pressure or blood thinning.

  2. 5 to 20 min

    The check-up

    The dentist looks at every tooth with a mirror and probe, checks the gums, and takes an X-ray only if something needs a closer look.

  3. 20 to 30 min

    Plan and cost, written down

    You get a sheet with the teeth that need work, what is urgent and what can wait, the number of sittings and the fee for each. Take it home. There is no pressure to decide today.

  4. 30 to 40 min

    Scaling or a quick fix, if you want

    Many people do the scaling on the first day. If a tooth is paining we give a dressing or start the treatment so you sleep tonight.

Common questions

Things people ask before they come.

Do I need an appointment?
It is better to book, because each dentist has one chair. Send a WhatsApp message or call. If you walk in, you may wait 20 to 40 minutes.
Does a root canal hurt?
The tooth is numbed before anything is done. You feel pressure and vibration, not pain. Mild soreness for a day or two after is normal and a paracetamol is usually enough.
I have diabetes or high blood pressure. Can I get an extraction?
Yes, in most cases. Bring your latest sugar or pressure reading and the list of medicines. If you take a blood thinner we will talk to your physician first.
Is scaling bad for teeth?
No. Scaling removes tartar from the surface; it does not thin the enamel. Teeth can feel sensitive for a few days afterwards because the gums settle back.
How do you sterilise instruments?
Every instrument set is washed, sealed in a pouch and run through the autoclave at 134 degrees. Pouches are opened in front of you. Needles, gloves and suction tips are single use.
Do you take health insurance?
We give a printed bill and treatment summary that most insurers accept for reimbursement. We do not do cashless.
