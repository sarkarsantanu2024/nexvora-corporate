A first consultation

Thirty minutes. Most of it is you talking.

  1. Before you come

    Note when the problem started, what makes it better or worse, and what you have already tried. Bring reports and current medicines. Do not stop any medicine on your own.

  2. The history

    The doctor asks about the complaint and then about you: sleep, appetite, thirst, sweat, weather you dislike, worries. In homeopathy these details choose the medicine. Give honest answers; nothing is odd here.

  3. Examination and plan

    A look at the skin, tongue, pulse and whatever the complaint needs. Then the doctor tells you what he thinks is going on, what he expects to change, and in how many weeks you should see something.

  4. Medicines and diet

    Medicines are given from the clinic dispensary in labelled bottles, with the dose written in Bengali and English. A one-page diet and routine sheet comes with ayurvedic treatment.

  5. Follow-up

    Usually after three to four weeks. If nothing has changed by the second follow-up, we say so plainly and talk about other options, including a referral.

Common questions

Asked at the desk most weeks.

How long before I see a change?
It depends on how long you have had the problem. A recent cold or a stomach upset may settle in days. Skin and allergy that have been there for years usually take months, and we tell you a realistic timeline on the first day. We cannot promise a result, and we will not pretend to.
Can I take these medicines with my allopathic ones?
In most cases yes, and we ask you to continue them. Bring the full list. If anything should be spaced out, the doctor writes the timing. Never stop a sugar, pressure, thyroid or heart medicine without your physician.
Do I have to give up tea, onion or garlic?
No. Keep a gap of fifteen minutes between the homeopathic medicine and strong tastes like coffee, mint or camphor. The ayurvedic diet sheet may ask you to cut down on some foods for a while; it says which and for how long.
Is homeopathy safe for babies and in pregnancy?
The medicines are given in very small doses and are widely used for both. Tell the doctor if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, and keep your obstetrician informed of anything you take.
Do you treat serious illness like cancer or kidney failure?
No. For those we support comfort alongside hospital treatment only if the treating doctor agrees. We do not take anyone off hospital treatment, and we say this clearly to any family who asks.
Do I need an appointment?
Yes, because a first visit takes half an hour. Message or call; same-week slots are usually available. Follow-ups can walk in during chamber hours and wait their turn.
