The studio

A workshop in the lane where idols have been made for 300 years

Nabakumar Pal came to Kumartuli from Krishnanagar in 1952 with one helper and a rented shed. His son Gopal built the studio's name in the club pujos of north Kolkata. Today his grandsons Sujit and Ranjit run it with twelve artisans, most of whom have been here over fifteen years.

We still use Ganga clay, rice straw and bamboo. We still paint the eyes on Mahalaya morning. What has changed is the ledger: orders now come by WhatsApp, and crates leave for Toronto and Dubai in July.

How it is made

From bamboo and straw to chokkhu daan

A 10 ft Durga takes about three months. Here is what happens in that time, in the order it happens.

  1. 1

    Kathamo Bamboo frame

    A bamboo skeleton is tied with jute rope on a wooden base. The proportions of the whole idol are fixed here, so this is the senior artisan's job.

  2. 2

    Khor bandha Straw body

    Rice straw is bound tightly over the frame to give the figure its bulk. It is light, cheap and lets the clay grip.

  3. 3

    Ek-mete First clay coat

    Coarse Ganga clay mixed with rice husk goes on thick. It dries for a week or two depending on the rain.

  4. 4

    Do-mete Fine clay coat

    A smoother clay with jute fibre is applied and the surface is worked by hand. Cracks are filled; fingers, folds and faces take shape.

  5. 5

    Mukh The face

    Faces are cast from the studio's own moulds, then finished by hand. Each family of artisans has its own face; ours is known for a slightly longer eye.

  6. 6

    Rong Painting

    A white chalk base, then the skin colour, then the detail work. We use water-based paints that wash off in immersion.

  7. 7

    Saaj Dressing and ornaments

    Sari, crown, weapons and the sholar or daker saaj go on. The lion gets his mane. This is the week the lane fills with visitors.

  8. 8

    Chokkhu daan Painting the eyes

    Done last, traditionally on Mahalaya, by the head of the family. The idol is considered awake from this moment and is handled with care until delivery.

Shipping abroad

Pujo in Toronto, London or Dubai, with an idol from this lane

Since 2008 we have shipped to Bengali associations in 14 countries. The idol is made in fibreglass or a light clay-and-fibre mix, packed in a wooden crate with foam, and handed to a freight agent we have worked with for years.

  • One point of contactSujit handles overseas orders himself, on WhatsApp, in Bengali or English. Photos at every stage.
  • Crating and paperworkExport-grade crate, fumigation certificate, invoice and packing list. Your freight agent or ours.
  • Sea or airSea freight takes 6 to 10 weeks and costs less. Air takes a week. We advise on which once we know your date.
  • Repaint serviceSend photos after the pujo and we send touch-up paint and instructions, or repaint the idol on your next visit to Kolkata.
