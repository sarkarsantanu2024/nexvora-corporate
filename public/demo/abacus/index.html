What abacus does for a child

The beads are only the first year. After that the child pictures the abacus in the head and moves the beads with the mind. That is what we are really teaching.

  • Concentration

    A child doing 10-digit sums in the head cannot think about anything else. Teachers tell us the same child sits still in school too.

  • Speed and accuracy

    By Level 4 most children add a column of fifteen two-digit numbers in under thirty seconds, without paper. School maths stops being a fear.

  • Both sides of the brain

    The right hand moves beads, the mind holds the picture. Visual and logical work together. Children also get better at remembering and at listening.

  • Confidence

    Every level ends with a certificate. Every year there is a competition hall full of children. Standing on a stage at seven does something that lasts.

Eight levels, one bead at a time

Each level takes about three to four months. There is a written and a speed exam at the end, with a certificate from our affiliating body. The whole course takes two and a half to three years.

  1. Level 1 Age 5+

    Holding the abacus, bead values, single-digit addition and subtraction on the beads. Lots of games.

  2. Level 2 Small friends

    Two-digit sums, the "small friend" and "big friend" formulas. First flash cards for mental pictures.

  3. Level 3 Mental starts

    Three-digit addition and subtraction. Child begins visualising: sums done with the abacus closed in the bag.

  4. Level 4 Multiplication

    Tables up to 9 on the abacus, 2-digit by 1-digit. Speed test: 50 sums in 5 minutes. First competition entry.

  5. Level 5 Division

    Division on the abacus, decimals introduced. Mental sums of 10 rows, 2 digits each.

  6. Level 6 Mixed sums

    All four operations mixed. Negative numbers. Mental multiplication of 2-digit by 2-digit.

  7. Level 7 Decimals

    Decimal multiplication and division, percentages. Mental sums 15 rows of 3 digits. Dictation sums.

  8. Level 8 Grand level

    Full speed and accuracy. Square roots. Children at this level sit for the national grand competition.

Questions parents ask

What is the right age to start?

Five to seven is ideal. The child should know numbers 1 to 100 and be able to sit for 45 minutes. We take children up to 14, but older children move through the levels faster and join a separate batch.

Will it clash with school maths?

No. Abacus gives the answer; school teaches the method. Children do school sums the school's way in their copies and use the mental picture to check. Teachers usually notice only that the child is faster.

How much practice at home?

Ten minutes a day, from the practice book. Not more. We would rather have ten minutes every day than an hour on Sunday. Parents do not need to know abacus to help; the book has answers.

Which abacus do you use?

The 17-rod Japanese soroban style, with one bead above and four below. It is the standard for all competitions. The starter kit includes one, and it lasts the whole course.

What if my child misses classes?

Tell us and the child can sit in another batch the same week. If a whole month is missed for illness or travel, we pause the fee for that month.

Is the certificate recognised?

Level certificates come from our affiliating abacus association and are the same ones accepted at the competitions. They are not a school-board qualification and we do not claim they are.
